Systems, in motion.

tl;dr Understanding how a system fails usually means reading a pile of blog posts. sim-xp turns the story into a short simulation you watch: components, dashboards and a live feed, pausing at the moments that explain it.

Play a simulation ↗
The problem
  • Postmortems and design posts are long, and the mechanism is spread across paragraphs.
  • Reading about a metric climbing is not the same as seeing it climb next to the alert and the log line.
  • After reading five summaries you still can't picture the system.
The bet
Watch

Seeing is cheaper than reading.

Watching a system move costs less effort than parsing prose. The feel and the ease are the point.

Story

Concepts and incidents are the same thing.

Every simulation is a narrated story: a trigger, a mechanism, a fix, a lesson.

Playbook

One file per story.

A playbook is plain JSON. The player plays it the same way every time, so a good one can be shared.

story→playbook (JSON)→run→report

How it works

The playbook
The initial state (components, dashboards, metrics, alerts), a timeline of events, and a report. A small required core, free-form text everywhere else.
The player
One generic page. Nobody operates anything: the engine plays the playbook deterministically. Space plays and pauses, ←/→ jump between key moments, R opens the report, H switches to Hardcore for the deeper layer.
The skill
story-to-playbook turns a blog post, postmortem, concept or short prompt into a validated playbook file. It writes that one file and nothing else.
The artifact
The whole app is a single built file, so it also runs as a Claude artifact with an in-page chat where Claude writes the playbook for you.

What it is not

  • Not a drill you operate. The first version (the MVP) was, and is deprecated.
  • Not a live system or a benchmark. The numbers are written to hold together, not measured.
  • Not non-deterministic yet. Branching choices and random runs come later, if the bet holds.
